How the simulation works

  • The needle shows angular distance from the selected course, not miles. Full scale is 10° either side, 2° per dot on a five-dot CDI (PHAK Ch. 16; IFH Ch. 9). That’s why the same offset in miles swings the needle harder near the station.
  • The TO/FROM flag depends only on which side you’re on of the line through the station perpendicular to the selected course. Heading never changes the CDI (PHAK Ch. 16).
  • Flying roughly opposite the selected course on a CDI gives reverse sensing. An HSI turns the course with the airplane, so the picture stays right side up (IFH Ch. 9).

Simplifications. These are choices made for the simulation, not FAA figures:

  • Cone of confusion is a fixed 0.75 NM radius. The real size depends on altitude and the station.
  • The flag drops within 2° of abeam the station. Real receivers vary.
  • No altitude, terrain, line-of-sight, or service volume limits (see AIM 1-1-8 for service volumes).
  • Flat map, magnetic north up, no magnetic variation, no station or receiver error.
  • The HSI is drawn with two dots each side, 5° per dot, full scale 10°. Check the G3X Touch Pilot’s Guide for exactly how the 100i displays it.
  • Wind is steady, and turns are standard rate (3° per second).
