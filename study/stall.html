Lift coefficient

Lift rises steadily with angle of attack until the critical angle of attack. Past it, the airflow separates, lift falls off, and drag climbs fast. This simulated wing stalls at 16°.

Key points

  • A wing stalls when it exceeds its critical angle of attack. That can happen at any airspeed, in any attitude, at any power setting.
  • Heavier weight, a steeper bank, or more G load raises the stall speed. The critical angle stays the same.
  • Separation starts at the trailing edge and creeps forward. You often feel it first as buffet, and the stall warning comes on a few knots before the stall.
  • To recover, reduce the angle of attack first. Then level the wings, add power as needed, and return to your flight path.
  • Trainer wings are designed so the root stalls before the tips, which keeps the ailerons working as the stall develops.

The model is an idealized airfoil, not a specific airplane. Real critical angles of attack are usually around 15° to 20°.
