Specs and limits

The numbers on this page are for the standard 160 hp 172R, serial numbers 17280001 and on, with the conventional instrument panel. They come from FAA TCDS 3A12 and the 172R Pilot's Operating Handbook (original issue December 2, 1996, through Revision 9 of July 19, 2004).

Two kinds of 172R use different books. Airplanes converted to 180 hp with Cessna kit MK172-72-01 use POH 172R180PH, and airplanes with the Garmin G1000 use their own AFM. If your airplane is one of those, or its POH has a later revision, its POH and placards win over anything here.

Fuel limits that come up on checkrides

  • Take off and land with the fuel selector on BOTH.
  • Running on LEFT or RIGHT alone is for level flight only.
  • With one tank dry, a slip or skid can last no more than 30 seconds.
  • With a quarter tank or less, don't fly uncoordinated for long on a single tank.
  • Fuel left in a tank once its gauge reads zero can't be used safely in flight.

V-speeds

Limit speeds are from POH Section 2 and match TCDS 3A12. Stall speeds are from POH Section 5 at 2,450 lb with power off. Other speeds are from POH Sections 3 and 4. The POH says Section 4 speeds are based on 2,450 lb and may be used at lower weights.

Learn these in KIAS, because that's what your airspeed indicator shows. KCAS corrects for instrument and position error. In this airplane the difference is large near the stall: the 30° flap stall is 33 KIAS but 47 KCAS. The POH notes that its KIAS stall values are approximate.

Systems

From POH Section 7 unless noted.

Engine and induction
The IO-360-L2A is fuel injected, so there is no carburetor and no carburetor heat. Air comes in through a filter at the front of the lower cowling. If the filter gets blocked, a spring-loaded alternate air door in the air box opens by itself and pulls unfiltered air from inside the cowling. The POH says to expect about 10 percent less power at full throttle on alternate air. Ignition is two magnetos, two spark plugs per cylinder, with an OFF, R, L, BOTH, START key switch.
Fuel system
Two vented integral tanks, one in each wing. The selector has LEFT, RIGHT, and BOTH, and there is a separate fuel shutoff valve that you push in for ON and pull out for OFF. The system includes a fuel strainer, an engine-driven fuel pump, a fuel/air control unit, a distribution valve, and an injection nozzle at each cylinder. An electric auxiliary fuel pump is used for priming before start and as a backup if the engine-driven pump fails. Sample fuel from the drains before each flight and after each fueling. The preflight checklist shows where each drain is.
Electrical
28-volt DC system with a belt-driven 60-amp alternator and a 24-volt battery on the left forward side of the firewall. Power goes through a split primary bus with an essential bus between the halves. An avionics master switch powers the avionics buses. The annunciator panel shows warnings for low fuel quantity, low oil pressure, low vacuum, and low voltage.
Flaps
Electric, single-slot flaps. The flap lever has stops at 10°, 20°, and 30°, and a pointer next to it shows flap position. Flaps are protected by a circuit breaker labeled FLAP.
Brakes
A single-disc hydraulic brake on each main wheel, worked by the tops of the rudder pedals. The pilot's and copilot's pedals are interconnected. The parking brake handle is under the left side of the panel: hold the pedals, pull the handle aft, and turn it 90° down.
Landing gear
Tricycle gear. The main gear legs are tubular spring steel, and the nose wheel is steerable. Wheel fairings are optional.
Pitot-static
The pitot tube is under the left wing and the static port is on the lower left side of the forward fuselage. Some airplanes have pitot heat. An alternate static source valve below the throttle switches the instruments to cabin air if the outside port is blocked. Cabin air pressure changes the readings a little, and POH Section 5 has the correction table.
Vacuum and gyros
The attitude indicator and heading indicator are vacuum driven. This 172R has two engine-driven vacuum pumps with check valves, so the gyros keep working if one pump fails. A low-vacuum switch on each pump feeds the annunciator. Set the heading indicator to the magnetic compass before takeoff and recheck it in flight.
Stall warning
A pneumatic horn fed by an inlet in the left wing's leading edge. It sounds 5 to 10 knots above the stall.

Things that surprise students

  • There's no primer knob. You prime with the auxiliary fuel pump and the mixture, and a warm engine skips priming. The POH has a separate note for starting a flooded engine.
  • The fuel shutoff valve is separate from the selector. Make sure it's pushed in before you start.
  • The static RPM range at full throttle is 2,065 to 2,165, well below the 2,400 RPM red line. That's normal for this fixed-pitch propeller.
  • The stall speeds in KIAS look very low, 33 KIAS with full flaps. The KCAS is 47, so most of that gap is airspeed indicator error near the stall.
  • The POH copy used here includes a temporary revision with a different starting procedure for airplanes that haven't had Service Bulletin SB04-24-01 done. Ask your instructor which one applies to your airplane.

Avionics

The POH lists this as the 172R's standard avionics:

  • KX-155A nav/com with a KI 208 or KI 209A indicator
  • KT-76C transponder
  • KMA-26 audio panel
  • Emergency locator transmitter
  • A clock, outside air temperature, and voltmeter display in the upper left panel

The POH describes conventional round-gauge flight instruments in a basic T: the gyros in the center, the airspeed indicator on the left, and the altimeter on the right. The tachometer and navigation instruments sit to the right. Fuel quantity, oil temperature and pressure, the vacuum gauge and ammeter, and the EGT and fuel flow gauge sit to the left. An annunciator panel above the altimeter shows warnings for fuel quantity, oil pressure, low vacuum, and low voltage.

Some 172Rs were built with the Garmin G1000 instead. Those airplanes use a separate AFM (part number 172RPHAUS, or 172RPHBUS with the GFC 700 autopilot), and the numbers on this page may not match them.

Installed avionics vary by airplane, and your instructor will add what's in each fleet airplane. Use the POH supplements in Section 9 for the equipment actually installed.

Procedures and flows

These are summaries to help you study. In the airplane, use the airplane's own checklist and POH Sections 3 and 4.

Engine start

Throttle open about a quarter inch, mixture at idle cutoff, area clear, master and beacon on. If the engine is cold, prime by turning on the auxiliary fuel pump, moving the mixture to full rich until fuel flow is steady for a few seconds, then back to idle cutoff and pump off. Crank, and advance the mixture smoothly once it fires. Check oil pressure right away. Skip priming on a warm engine. Airplanes without SB04-24-01 have a slightly different start flow in the POH temporary revision. See POH Section 4, Starting Engine.

Runup items specific to the 172R

  • Run up at 1,800 RPM.
  • Magneto check: no more than 150 RPM drop on either magneto, and no more than 50 RPM difference between them.
  • Check the vacuum gauge, engine instruments, and ammeter, and make sure no annunciators are lit.
  • Fuel selector on BOTH, mixture rich, flaps set 0° to 10° for takeoff.

See POH Section 4, Before Takeoff.

Takeoff and landing flaps

  • Normal takeoff uses 0° to 10° of flaps. Lift the nose wheel at 55 KIAS and climb at 70 to 80 KIAS.
  • Short field takeoff uses 10° of flaps, brakes held during full power, 57 KIAS until clear of obstacles, then flaps up slowly after 60 KIAS.
  • Above 3,000 ft, lean the mixture for maximum RPM before takeoff.
  • Normal landing flaps are as desired. 10° is allowed below 110 KIAS and 10° to 30° below 85 KIAS.
  • Short field landing uses full 30° flaps at 62 KIAS, power to idle after clearing the obstacle, then heavy braking.
  • For a go-around, go to full power and bring the flaps up to 20° right away, then climb at 55 KIAS. Bring the flaps to 10° until obstacles are cleared, and fully up once you're at a safe altitude and 60 KIAS.

See POH Section 4, Takeoff and Landing.

Emergency memory items

The POH prints its immediate-action items in bold. In summary:

  • Engine failure on the takeoff roll: throttle to idle and brake.
  • Engine failure right after takeoff: pitch for 65 KIAS with flaps up or 60 KIAS with flaps down, and land more or less straight ahead. Then shut off fuel, ignition, and master if time allows.
  • Engine failure in flight: pitch for 65 KIAS, best glide. Check fuel shutoff ON and selector BOTH, turn on the auxiliary pump, mixture rich, and check the ignition switch. A windmilling propeller usually restarts on its own.
  • Fire during start: keep cranking so the engine pulls the fire in. If it doesn't start, keep cranking with full throttle and mixture at idle cutoff, fuel shutoff off and auxiliary pump off.
  • Engine fire in flight: mixture to idle cutoff, fuel shutoff off, auxiliary pump off, master off. Then cabin heat and air off, 100 KIAS, and a forced landing.
  • Electrical fire in flight: master off, vents and cabin heat closed, use the extinguisher. Ventilate the cabin once the fire is out.

See POH Section 3 for the full procedures, including the electrical, vacuum, icing, and static source failures.
