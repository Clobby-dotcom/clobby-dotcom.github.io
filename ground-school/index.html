Ground school

Everything on the private pilot knowledge test, one topic at a time. Go in order the first time through, then come back to whatever you need.

The airplane

  1. AerodynamicsThe four forces, angle of attack, stalls, load factor, stability, and left-turning tendencies.PHAK Ch. 4–5
  2. Flight controlsPrimary and secondary controls, trim, and what each one actually does to the airplane.PHAK Ch. 6
  3. Aircraft systemsEngine, propeller, induction and carburetor ice, fuel, oil, ignition, and electrical systems.PHAK Ch. 7
  4. Flight instrumentsPitot-static and gyroscopic instruments, the compass, and how each one fails.PHAK Ch. 8
  5. Documents, inspections, and equipmentWhat has to be on board, which inspections are due, and flying with inoperative equipment.PHAK Ch. 9; 14 CFR 91
  6. Weight and balanceDatum, arm, moment, and CG, with a step-by-step loading walkthrough.PHAK Ch. 10
  7. PerformanceDensity altitude, takeoff and landing distance, climb, cruise, and reading the POH charts.PHAK Ch. 11

Flying in the system

  1. Airport operationsTraffic patterns, runway markings, signs, lighting, and communicating at towered and nontowered airports.PHAK Ch. 14; AIM Ch. 4
  2. AirspaceClass A through G, special use airspace, and the weather minimums for each.PHAK Ch. 15; AIM Ch. 3
  3. RegulationsThe parts of 14 CFR 61 and 91 a private pilot lives by, plus accident reporting under NTSB 830.14 CFR 61, 91; 49 CFR 830
  4. NavigationSectional charts, pilotage, dead reckoning, VOR, and GPS.PHAK Ch. 16

Weather

  1. Weather theoryPressure, stability, air masses, fronts, clouds, fog, icing, and thunderstorms.PHAK Ch. 12
  2. Weather servicesReading METARs, TAFs, and the other reports and forecasts you'll use in a briefing.PHAK Ch. 13; AC 00-45

The pilot

  1. Aeromedical factorsHypoxia, spatial disorientation, carbon monoxide, alcohol and medication, and fitness to fly.PHAK Ch. 17; AIM Ch. 8
  2. Aeronautical decision-makingRisk management, hazardous attitudes, and the checklists that keep you out of trouble.PHAK Ch. 2
  3. Cross-country planningPutting it all together: route, nav log, fuel, weather, and flight plans.14 CFR 91.103; PHAK Ch. 16

For study only. In the airplane, the POH and current FAA publications always take priority.
